WARS Weighted Asset Risk Score

Sorted by CVSS alone, a vulnerability list quickly becomes a wall of “criticals”: two 9.8s look identical. WARS adds what CVSS deliberately leaves out — how likely exploitation is and how much the affected asset matters — to answer one question: what do we fix first on Monday morning?

I designed and built it end to end in Python during my internship, within the asset-based approach of ISO/IEC 27005. Each asset gets a 0–100 score, a P1–P5 priority band and a factor-by-factor breakdown that can be defended in front of an auditor.

Try the calculator Compare the rankings

Covered by a confidentiality agreement: this page stays at a public level and every example uses fictional values.

Five questions, one score

WARS = (S × L × E × AC × BI) ÷ N × 100

S Severity
How severe is the flaw? CVSS ÷ 10 · NVD
L Likelihood
How likely is it to be exploited in the next 30 days? EPSS · 0 – 1
E Exposure
How exposed is the asset? discovery step · 0.2 – 0.8
AC Asset criticality
How important is the asset? classification step · 1 – 2
BI Business impact
What would an incident cost the organisation? ISO/IEC 27005 criteria · 1 – 2

KEV Known to be exploited (CISA KEV)? Then the score never drops below 80, whatever the other factors say.

N = 3.2 is the highest possible product, so the score runs from 0 to 100.

03 / The prototype

From a whole domain to a ranked shortlist

WARS is a pipeline of four standalone steps. Each one hands validated JSON (Pydantic) to the next, so any step can be replayed and audited on its own.

  1. 01

    Discover

    What is visible from the Internet?

    Passive crawl only: GET and HEAD requests, robots.txt respected, conservative rate limits.

    httpx · asyncio · defusedxml

  2. 02

    Classify

    What does each asset do?

    Information, business process or both — and how sensitive. The only step where an LLM is used.

    LangGraph · LLM · rule layer

  3. 03

    Assess

    What could happen, and how bad?

    Findings enriched with CVSS from NVD, EPSS exploitation likelihood, CISA KEV and MITRE ATT&CK techniques.

    NVD · EPSS · KEV · ATT&CK

  4. 04

    Report

    What do we fix first?

    Self-contained HTML/PDF report ranked P1–P5, every score broken down factor by factor, TLP 2.0 marked.

    Playwright · HTML · PDF

04 / Design decisions

Choices I stand by — and limits I state

  • The model never touches a number. The LLM only answers “what is this asset?”. Factors, formula and thresholds are deterministic, so every score can be justified line by line — and the tool still runs on its rules without an API key.
  • Multiply, don’t add. If one factor is close to zero the score must collapse: a severe flaw on an asset with no business value should not top the list.
  • Known exploitation is a floor. A CISA KEV match keeps the score in the critical band, whatever the other factors say.
  • Uncertainty counts as risk. A doubtful classification, a failed step or a missing source sends the asset to human review — never silently to low priority.

What I don’t claim: the weighting is argued, not yet statistically validated. The next step is to calibrate it against real remediation decisions.

What it doesn’t cover: unpublished flaws, misconfigurations without a CVE, internal assets and human or organisational risks. WARS is a decision-support tool, not a scanner.
